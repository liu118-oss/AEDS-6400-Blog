# Blog Post 4: Housing prices, mortgage rates, and affordability

## Research question

How have home prices and mortgage rates jointly changed the affordability of buying a typical new U.S. home since 2000?

## Data

The analysis downloads four series programmatically from FRED's public graph CSV endpoint. No API key or manual data download is required.

| Series | Description | Original frequency | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| `MSPUS` | Median sales price of new houses sold | Quarterly | Census Bureau and HUD |
| `MORTGAGE30US` | 30-year fixed mortgage rate | Weekly | Freddie Mac |
| `CPIAUCSL` | Consumer Price Index | Monthly | Bureau of Labor Statistics |
| `MEHOINUSA646N` | Median household income | Annual | Census Bureau |

The project uses 2000–2025 because 2025 is the latest complete year shared by all four series. FRED data can be revised, so rerunning the code later may produce small differences from the committed results.

## Repository structure

- `code/01_analyze_housing.R`: downloads, cleans, transforms, analyzes, and visualizes the data.
- `data/raw/`: CSV files downloaded from FRED plus a retrieval note.
- `data/processed/annual_housing_affordability.csv`: analysis-ready annual data.
- `results/`: three figures and a headline-results table generated by the script.
- `index.qmd`: Quarto blog post.

## Reproduce

From the repository root:

```sh
Rscript -e 'install.packages(c("dplyr", "tidyr", "readr", "ggplot2", "scales", "knitr"))'
Rscript posts/week-04/code/01_analyze_housing.R
quarto render
```

Running the analysis requires an internet connection because it refreshes all four FRED CSV files.

## Transformations and assumptions

1. Quarterly home prices, weekly mortgage rates, and monthly CPI are converted to annual averages.
2. Home prices are converted to 2025 dollars using annual average CPI: `nominal price × 2025 CPI / current-year CPI`.
3. The illustrative buyer makes a 20% down payment and finances the remainder with a 30-year fixed-rate mortgage.
4. Payment burden equals monthly principal and interest divided by median annual household income divided by 12.
5. The counterfactual uses each year's home price and income but fixes the mortgage rate at its 2021 annual average.

The payment estimate excludes property taxes, homeowners insurance, mortgage insurance, maintenance, closing costs, and differences across places or households. It is a descriptive national benchmark rather than personalized financial advice or a mortgage-approval calculation.
