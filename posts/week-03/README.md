# Blog Post 3: Unemployment by education in the IPUMS CPS

## Research question

How does unemployment vary by educational attainment among U.S. adults ages 25-64, and does the education gap change when the labor market weakens?

## Required IPUMS extract

IPUMS requires each user to register and download their own extract. At [IPUMS CPS](https://cps.ipums.org/cps/):

1. Select **Basic Monthly** samples for **April of every year, 2000-2025**. Do not select the ASEC supplement.
2. Select a rectangular person-level extract.
3. Include `YEAR`, `MONTH`, `AGE`, `EDUC`, `LABFORCE`, `EMPSTAT`, and `WTFINL`.
4. Download the matching `.xml` DDI and `.dat.gz` data files.
5. Put both files in `posts/week-03/data/raw/` without renaming or decompressing them.

April is used consistently across years. This provides one cross-section per year, captures the sharp April 2020 disruption, and avoids combining repeated monthly interviews. The analysis is limited to civilians ages 25-64 who are in the labor force.

## Repository structure

- `code/01_analyze_cps.R`: imports the IPUMS extract, validates its variables and samples, applies `WTFINL`, produces summary files, and saves all figures.
- `data/raw/`: local IPUMS extract; ignored by Git and not redistributed.
- `data/processed/`: weighted statistics generated by the analysis.
- `results/`: figures and a small headline-results table generated by the analysis.
- `index.qmd`: Quarto article.

## Reproduce

From the repository root:

```sh
Rscript -e 'install.packages(c("ipumsr", "dplyr", "tidyr", "readr", "haven", "ggplot2", "scales", "knitr"))'
Rscript posts/week-03/code/01_analyze_cps.R
quarto render
```

The raw microdata are excluded from Git. The processed aggregate tables and figures should be committed so the published article is reproducible without redistributing the IPUMS person-level file.

## Weighting and definitions

Every unemployment rate is calculated as `sum(WTFINL * unemployed) / sum(WTFINL)` among people in the civilian labor force. `LABFORCE == 2` identifies the labor force; detailed `EMPSTAT` codes 20-22 identify unemployment. Education is grouped into less than high school, high school/GED, some college/associate degree, and bachelor's degree or higher. These are descriptive population estimates. The project does not calculate standard errors or make causal claims.
